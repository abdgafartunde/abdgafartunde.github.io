Before applying for the Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme, I had spent the previous year applying to universities in the United States and Europe. Some applications were unsuccessful; others offered partial funding or no funding. I was looking for full financial support to pursue a PhD and had not yet found it.

A friend asked to see my CV because he wanted to understand why I had not secured the funding I needed. He was enthusiastic about my qualifications, encouraged me to consider HKPFS, and urged me to prepare carefully for the next application cycle.

I applied in 2020, received the fellowship in April 2021, and began my PhD at The Chinese University of Hong Kong in August 2021, supervised by Professor Jun Zou. I remain deeply grateful for the fellowship, his support, and my experience at CUHK. From my experience, I regard HKPFS as one of the best funding opportunities available to a PhD student.

This article brings together my application experience and the advice I would give another applicant. Current application information concerns **2027/28 entry** and was checked on **9 November 2026**.

## What HKPFS considers

Administered by Hong Kong's Research Grants Council, HKPFS supports new full-time PhD students at eight participating universities. It includes a stipend and conference and research-related travel support for up to three years; universities may provide additional support for longer PhDs. Check the [RGC scheme information](https://cerg1.ugc.edu.hk/hkpfs/index.html) and your university's terms for the relevant intake.

The RGC identifies academic excellence as its primary consideration, alongside research ability and potential, communication and interpersonal skills, and leadership. Your application should help a reader understand these qualities through specific evidence of your work and experience. [RGC selection criteria](https://cerg1.ugc.edu.hk/hkpfs/index.html).

## Applying with a bachelor's degree

I applied with a first-class bachelor's degree in Mathematics from the Federal University of Technology, Minna, Nigeria. I had not completed a master's degree. Most HKPFS recipients I knew had already completed research master's degrees, so receiving the fellowship with my undergraduate qualification was particularly meaningful to me.

In my view, a first-class degree can strengthen an application. I also had several peer-reviewed publications by the time I applied, and I believe my academic record and research experience helped my application. Alhamdulillah.

For applicants with a bachelor's degree, CUHK's current general requirements include bachelor's-entry routes for the PhD stream of MPhil–PhD programmes, subject to academic conditions. Check the requirements of your chosen programme. [CUHK admissions requirements](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/requirements).

## Finding a supervisor and making contact

The international standing of Hong Kong's universities first caught my attention. CUHK's reputation and the strength of its mathematics faculty encouraged me to look more closely at possible supervisors. I searched for researchers whose work connected with my background and the directions I wanted to explore.

That search led me to Professor Jun Zou. At the time, I was interested in physics-informed neural networks for computational fluid dynamics problems, within scientific machine learning. I read some of his recent papers, paying attention to open questions and suggested future work, then read related papers addressing similar problems. That helped me develop ideas for a possible project.

I contacted him about a month before the application round opened. My first email introduced my experience, outlined my proposed research and its connection with his work, and expressed my interest in working with him. I attached my CV and a student copy of my transcript.

His first response was encouraging. We exchanged many emails about my background, publications, awards, research interests, and proposal. His comments and recommendations helped me improve the application. Starting early gave me time to address questions and revise my materials.

My advice is to read a prospective supervisor's work before writing. Explain a specific research connection, the experience that prepares you to pursue it, and why you want to work with that person. Attach a clear CV and follow any contact instructions provided by the supervisor or department.

## Completing the RGC and university applications

For **2027/28 entry**, the RGC initial application opened on 1 September 2026 at noon Hong Kong time and closes on **1 December 2026 at 12:00 noon Hong Kong time**. There are two stages:

1. **RGC initial application:** obtain an HKPFS reference number. The current round allows up to two programme or department choices at one or two universities.
2. **Full university applications:** apply separately to the selected universities, quoting that reference number and meeting their requirements and deadlines.

Completing the initial application alone does not complete the process. [RGC application instructions](https://cerg1.ugc.edu.hk/hkpfs/apply.html).

CUHK's current full-application deadline is **1 December 2026 at 11:59 pm Hong Kong time**, later than the noon RGC deadline. If you select two CUHK programmes, CUHK requires separate applications for each. [CUHK HKPFS instructions](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/scholarships-fees/hkpfs).

### The additional HKPFS material at CUHK

Within the university stage, my CUHK application involved two parts:

- **General admission information:** the information required for the chosen programme, including the RGC-issued HKPFS reference number where requested.
- **Additional HKPFS material:** the research proposal, essay responses, and details of publications, previous awards, community service, and extracurricular activities. Referee information also had to be provided through the university system.

Allow time to complete the full application, including supporting documents and any actions required after submitting the general information. My description reflects my application experience; follow the current portal for the exact sequence.

The reference number is essential. The RGC states that a university application without it will be processed as a regular application, without HKPFS consideration. [RGC application FAQs](https://cerg1.ugc.edu.hk/hkpfs/enquiry.html).

## Writing the proposal and essays well

**I regard preparing and writing the additional HKPFS material well as one of the most important parts of the application.** Give yourself time to develop your ideas, answer the questions carefully, seek feedback, and revise.

My proposal grew out of reading Professor Zou's papers and related work. A future-work section can suggest a starting question, but follow it into the literature: another paper may already have addressed it, or the proposed extension may involve substantial difficulties.

A proposal should explain the problem, why it matters, what existing work achieves, and what you would initially investigate. Show how your background prepares you to begin and how the direction connects with the supervisor's expertise. A focused, realistic question gives the reader something concrete to assess.

For the essays, answer the actual prompt and observe its word or character limit. Use specific examples to explain your motivation, preparation, or future plans. State your contribution to publications and describe their status accurately. For awards, identify the awarding body, date, and relevant context. For community service and extracurricular activities, explain your responsibilities and what you did.

Review the material as a whole. Names, dates, publication details, and other claims should agree with your CV and supporting documents. Make the writing clear and organized, and ensure that you can explain everything you submit. Professor Zou's feedback helped me improve my application; I would encourage another applicant to take revision just as seriously.

## Choosing and supporting your referees

**Referee reports deserve serious attention too.** Ask people who know your academic work well whether they can provide a strong, detailed, and truthful assessment of your abilities and research potential.

I was already in regular contact with my referees, who had supported previous applications. For HKPFS, I updated them on my latest publications and sent a current CV so that they had an accurate picture of my academic development.

Referees can be very busy. Ask early, explain why the application matters to you, and respectfully ask whether they have time to write a comprehensive report. A few generic lines may leave out achievements and qualities they could describe through specific examples. Their assessment should remain their own honest judgment.

Provide your updated CV, recent achievements, proposed research, submission instructions, and deadlines. Discuss developments they may have missed, especially if they last wrote for you some time ago. Keep in touch and send courteous reminders so they have time to complete the report thoughtfully.

CUHK's current general document guidance specifies **three referee reports for research doctoral applications**. Follow the university and programme instructions for arranging them. [CUHK documents required](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/documents-required).

## Have evidence for what you claim

**For every qualification or achievement you include, be prepared to provide evidence.** If you list a professional certification, have the certificate or official verification ready. The same principle applies to degrees, awards, publications, and other achievements: describe them accurately and keep the relevant records organized.

Prepare readable copies early. You may be asked to upload documents, arrange official submission, or send evidence to the programme or Graduate School by email when instructed. Respond through the specified channel and by the stated deadline; outstanding evidence can delay consideration or leave admission conditions unmet.

Distinguish informal contact from formal admission requirements. I sent a student transcript with my first email to Professor Zou. CUHK currently requires copies of official transcripts and grading schemes at the application stage, with original-document verification later. It may also request additional documentary proof. [CUHK document and verification guidance](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/documents-required).

In my case, follow-up requests after submission included an official transcript, referee recommendations, and official confirmation that my undergraduate degree had been taught in English. CUHK currently lists a university Registry letter confirming English as the medium of instruction among accepted language evidence. Check the requirements that apply to your programme. [CUHK admissions requirements](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/requirements).

## Preparing for an interview

I did not have a separate interview. Professor Zou and I had already discussed my background and proposed research extensively by email; at times, the exchanges were frequent enough to feel like a chat.

Check your own programme's arrangements. If invited to an interview, prepare to explain your academic background, the work on your CV, your research interests, and their connection with the programme. Your responses should be consistent with your application, and you should be able to explain your contribution to any paper or project you mention.

## How my offers arrived

After submitting in December 2020, I received the decisions over several months, all by email:

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| About a month before the 2020 round opened | I first contacted Professor Zou. |
| December 2020 | I submitted my application. |
| Late January 2021 | CUHK offered admission with a postgraduate studentship. |
| February 2021 | I received the CUHK Vice-Chancellor's award. |
| April 2021 | I received HKPFS. |
| August 2021 | I began my PhD at CUHK. |

The initially offered postgraduate studentship was not part of my final package once I received HKPFS and the Vice-Chancellor's award. Admission and the funding decisions arrived separately. Read each offer carefully to understand what has been awarded, how awards fit together, and what remains pending. This timeline describes my experience.

## What my funding covered

My final package combined HKPFS and the CUHK Vice-Chancellor's award:

- **Stipend:** HKPFS covered the first three years; the Vice-Chancellor's award covered the fourth year.
- **Tuition:** the Vice-Chancellor's award covered my tuition throughout the PhD. I paid no tuition fees.
- **Accommodation:** the Vice-Chancellor's award covered my first year in the postgraduate hall.

The funding gave me financial security and supported conference participation and research visits. These opportunities, alongside the academic environment and my supervisor's support, are important reasons why I speak so positively about my time at CUHK.

Applicants should read the [current CUHK funding package and conditions](https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/scholarships-fees/hkpfs) for their intake rather than assume every detail of my package will apply to them.

## Final application checklist

Check that you have:

- Read the programme's admission requirements and completed both the RGC and university application stages, using the correct HKPFS reference number.
- Completed the additional HKPFS material, including the research proposal and essays, and revised it for clarity, accuracy, and compliance with the stated limits.
- Prepared evidence for the qualifications and achievements you list, with consistent details across the application and CV.
- Arranged referees who can provide detailed, truthful reports and supplied their current contact information as instructed.
- Checked document and referee-report deadlines, retained submission confirmations, and arranged to monitor email for follow-up requests.

My experience began with disappointing funding outcomes and a friend who encouraged me to try another opportunity. Reading carefully, finding a research fit, making contact early, and responding to feedback helped me prepare. I hope this account encourages another applicant to take their own preparation seriously and consider what HKPFS might make possible for them.
